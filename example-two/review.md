# Review: Hiring Panel Debrief Output

I scored [output.md](output.md) against what this transcript was designed to test. It's the opposite of [the first example](../example/): can the skill recognise a real decision, not just refuse a fake one?

## What Worked

It called the Jamie Ellis offer a real decision. Both panel members gave a clear yes. The output states it plainly as confirmed, without the hedging the first example gave the launch date. Treating a real agreement as unclear just because the skill leans towards caution would be a failure of its own. It didn't do that here.

It saw that deciding to wait is still a decision. The panel didn't decide Alex Rowe's outcome. They did decide, outright, on a process first: a second interview. The output records that as a confirmed decision in its own right, not an open item. That's the more exact reading of what happened.

It kept who owns an action apart from when it will happen. Jordan owning the second interview is confirmed. The "early next week" timing isn't, since Jordan said "aim for," and didn't commit. The output labels these separately, rather than treating the whole action as equally firm or equally soft.

It still flagged the one real non-decision. The take-home test idea got the same non-decision label as the first example's launch date. So the skill didn't swing the other way and call everything a decision just because two things in this meeting were.

## What Still Needs a Human Check

"Early next week" is vague. Someone reading this should get a date from Jordan, rather than let a soft target quietly become the working date.

If the second interview changes the picture on Alex Rowe, this will need a new write-up. It only reflects this one meeting.

## Verdict

No automatic failure. I built this example to test the opposite risk from the first one. A tool tuned only to catch false decisions could easily swing the other way and treat everything as unclear. This one didn't. It called the two real decisions decisions, and still called the one non-decision a non-decision. It also labelled the action with soft timing as softer than the fully confirmed one next to it.
